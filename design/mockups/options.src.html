How the matching copes with winding roads

The same method run on both areas, 2021 to 2025. West Berkshire is 70% rural; Reading 5%. Each collision on an A-road or motorway is matched to the nearest stretch of the same road, using DfT's straight lines or the stretches rebuilt along the real road from OS Open Roads.

On winding roads the rebuilt shapes matter far more. The M4 across West Berkshire went from 0.8 to 2.7 injury collisions per 100 million vehicle miles on one 18.6 km stretch, because the straight line between its ends runs more than half a kilometre from the motorway in places. Two things helped the rebuild: snapping a stretch's ends up to 2.5 km away (DfT's M4 ends sit out at junctions such as Chieveley), and letting the path cross short pieces of road that OS gives no number, such as roundabouts, at four times their length. Without them, 34 of the 47 stretches rebuilt.
